The Original Visualization : Finviz S&P 500 Map

The Finviz S&P 500 Map is a treemap of every company in the S&P 500. Each company is a tile: tile size encodes market capitalization, tile color encodes the daily percent change in stock price (green up, red down), and tiles are grouped by sector and industry. It gives traders a dense, at-a-glance picture of how the market moved on a given day, but the day's most important number, percent change, is carried only by color, while the eye is drawn to tile size. Visualization Redesign

The redesign keeps market capitalization on treemap area, which is the right channel for a value that spans trillions of dollars. Each company's daily percent change moves onto bar length and position measured from a zero line, which is a far more precise channel for reading and ranking a signed value. The treemap and the sector bar chart are linked. Hovering a sector in one highlights it in the other, and clicking a sector drills both views down to that sector's individual companies, with a breadcrumb back to the full market. A colorblind-safe palette swaps the usual red/green scheme for blue/orange, so sign is never carried by color alone. Bar direction and a signed percent label always confirm it. The data is a single-day snapshot of the S&P 500 retrieved September 24, 2026 combining Nasdaq market data with GICS sector classifications for 498 companies (2 of the S&P 500's 500 constituents are missing from our dataset). See Data Sources and References below.

Two controls sit above the visualizations. The sector average toggle switches between cap-weighted and equal-weighted daily change. Cap-weighted gives each company influence proportional to its market capitalization, which is how the S&P 500 index itself is calculated, so it shows what actually moved the index. Equal-weighted treats every company the same regardless of size, revealing whether a sector's move is broad-based or driven by just a few giants. Comparing the two is often the most telling part of the story. A sector can look flat on a cap-weighted basis while most of its individual stocks moved sharply in one direction. The color scheme toggle switches between the default red/green palette and the colorblind-safe blue/orange palette described above.
