Two mappings of the same four variables — population (ratio), temp_c (interval), development_level (ordinal), region (nominal). Hover a point or bar to identify the city.

Visualization 1

Design Justification

I mapped population and temperature to the x- and y-axes because both are quantitative variables, and position on a common scale which helps viewers compare exact magnitudes and spot correlation between population and temperature. Region is nominal with no inherent order, so I used color, a channel with no implied ranking, to group cities categorically. Development level is ordinal, so I encoded it as size, which gives a natural sense of more/less. Size and color are also easy to distinguish at a glance in a dataset this small, since there are only three development levels and four regions to track, so the encoding stays readable without becoming cluttered.

Together, this lets a viewer ask questions like are larger, more developed cities clustered by region or and does population or temperature relate to development level? Color groups regions and size signals development tier without needing to check the legend for every point.

Visualization 2

Design Justification

For this version I have a grouped bar chart, split into one small panel per region. Development level sits on the x-axis because it is ordinal, so its Low/Medium/High order is preserved directly by the layout. Population, which is a ratio variable drives bar height since length is the visual channel people read most accurately for quantities. Temperature, an interval variable, is encoded as a sequential color ramp from light to dark, which suits continuous data well.

Splitting the chart into one panel per region uses grouping instead of a position or shape channel, which keeps region comparisons simple. Thus, a viewer can scan one panel to see how population and temperature vary across development levels within that region, or compare panels to see whether, for example, northern cities tend to be more developed than southern ones.

I expect viewers to notice that bar height and color don't always move together, suggesting population, temperature, and development level are fairly independent of each other.
