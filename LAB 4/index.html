Dataset: Twitter US Airline Sentiment

Source: Crowdflower/Figure Eight's Twitter US Airline Sentiment dataset (CC0, publicly redistributable), 14,640 tweets from February 2015.
Method: Bulk download of the public CSV, cleaned with pandas, then scored with a Twitter-trained RoBERTa sentiment model (cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest) run locally in Python.
Records used: 1,200 tweets, randomly sampled after cleaning from the 14,427 usable raw rows.
Cleaning performed: Dropped columns that were over 90% missing (coordinates, timezone, negative-reason fields), removed 155 duplicate tweet IDs and 213 duplicate texts, parsed timestamps to real datetimes, and stripped URLs/HTML entities from tweet text.

Analysis

This visualization is a stacked bar chart built from 1,200 tweets sampled from the Crowdflower Twitter US Airline Sentiment dataset (14,640 tweets in total), collected in February 2015. After downloading the raw CSV, I dropped columns that were over 90% missing (tweet_coord, user_timezone, negativereason), removed 155 duplicate tweet IDs and 213 duplicate tweet texts, parsed tweet_created into real datetimes, and stripped URLs and HTML entities from the tweet text.

Sentiment was estimated per tweet using cardiffnlp's Twitter-trained RoBERTa model which outputs a predicted label (negative/neutral/positive) with a confidence score for each class. These are model estimates and they agree with the dataset's original human-labeled sentiment 81.4% of the time. Each bar represents one airline, and the stacked segments encode the share of tweets classified as negative, neutral, or positive for that airline, letting all six airlines be compared directly on a common 0-100% scale. American and US Airways carry the largest negative shares of 69.1% and 72.2% respectively, United follows at 63.5%, while Virgin America stands out with a far more balanced mix of 33.3% negative, 35.6% neutral, 31.1% positive.
