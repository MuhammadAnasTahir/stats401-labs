1. Dataset Description

The dataset describes a fabricated commercial network of 12 companies across 7 sectors and 3 regions. Company data comes from data/lab7_assignment_companies.csv. Each company belongs to one sector such as manufacturing, logistics, retail, food, technology, wholesale or material. Regions include Asia, Europe or North America. Four companies belong to each region.

Transaction data comes from data/lab7_assignment_transactions_60days.csv, which records 368 transactions across 60 days, from January 1 to March 1, 2026. Each transaction links two companies and reports a dollar amount, a transaction type and a transaction count. Transaction type is one of five categories: services, goods, shipping, components or materials. Transaction amounts range from about 7,330 dollars to about 33,890 dollars. On a typical day, between 3 and 10 of the 27 company pairs that trade at some point have an active transaction. Links are treated as undirected for this visualization.

2. Temporal Network Visualization

Hover a node (company) for its sector, region and current transaction volume. Hover a link for its two companies, amount, type and date. Use play, pause, reset or the slider to move through the 60 days. Zoom functionality could be used to inspect nodes and links closely.

3. Design Description

Sector is encoded through node fill color since sector is the main category a reader would want to scan at a glance and color is the clearest channel for seven categories. Region is encoded through node border so it does not compete with sector. Asia gets a solid border, Europe a dashed border and North America no border. Current transaction volume is encoded through node radius and is recalculated every day from the links active that day so a company can grow or shrink from one frame to the next. A company with no active link on a given day is drawn small and dimmed, rather than removed, which keeps every company visible across all 60 days.

Transaction amount is encoded through link width. Transaction type is encoded through line style so the visualization never uses color for two different categorical variables at once. Services is solid, goods is dashed, shipping is dotted, components is a dash-dot pattern and materials is a long dash.

A force-directed network can rearrange itself completely from one frame to the next if the simulation is rebuilt each time which makes it hard for a viewer to track which circle is which company as the day changes. The 12 company nodes are created once when the page loads and reused for all 60 day so no node ever loses its identity. Each link is bound to its source and target company pair as a key so a relationship that stays active across two consecutive days reuses the same line element instead of being removed and redrawn and only a relationship that truly appears or disappears triggers a fade. Changing the day only restarts the existing force simulation at a low alpha rather than creating a new one so nodes drift gradually toward their next layout instead of snapping to new positions. These choices preserve the viewer mental map of where each company sits from one day to the next.

Links join and leave the network as the day changes. A new link fades in and a link that is no longer active fades out and is removed, which makes the change itself visible instead of hiding it inside an instant cut. A play, pause and reset control and a 60-day slider let a reader either watch the network evolve or stop on a specific day to inspect it, and a running summary reports the active company count, active link count and total transaction value for the current day.

4. Temporal Network Questions and Findings

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?
Dragging the slider across the 60 days shows connectivity rising unevenly rather than smoothly. Early days such as Day 1, Day 7 and Day 19 each show only 3 active links and 6 active companies in the summary line with a total transaction value under 50,000 dollars. By Day 45 the summary line reads 10 active links, 12 active companies and a total value of about 196,000 dollars, and Day 60 still shows 9 active links with all 12 companies active and a total of about 178,000 dollars. If the animation is played, the network starts as a few small and disconnected pairs and becomes a single denser web by the later days, with noticeably more and thicker links on screen.
2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?
Hovering BlueRiver Logistics on different days shows its activity swinging sharply rather than rising steadily. On Day 1 and Day 40 it has no active link and appears small and dimmed, while on Day 20 and Day 60 it shows 2 active links with a tooltip volume of about 26,500 and 35,400 dollars. Ion Systems ends larger, with 1 link and about 20,500 dollars on Day 1 versus 2 links and about 50,700 dollars on Day 60, directly visible as a bigger circle when comparing the two days. Companies such as Cedar Retail also pick up new connections by Day 45 and Day 60 that were not present in early January, visible as new links fading in around a node that previously had only one neighbor.
3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?
Yes, and this is visible just by counting the separate clumps of nodes on screen at different points on the slider. Day 1, Day 7 and Day 19 each show the network broken into many small, disconnected pieces, mostly single isolated companies plus 3 small pairs. By Day 30 the picture consolidates into fewer, larger clumps, and by Day 45 and Day 60 nearly the entire network reads as one connected mass with only a couple of companies still separate. Moving slowly from an early day to a later day makes this consolidation visible as disconnected groups gradually merging through newly appearing links.
4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?
Hovering the same link on two different days it is active shows some relationships growing noticeably stronger. The Fusion Electronics to Granite Wholesale link is thin and solid on Day 20, with a tooltip amount of about 8,000 dollars and type services, but from Day 22 onward the same link appears visibly thicker and dash-dot, with a tooltip amount above 20,000 dollars and type components. The Ion Systems to Juniper Stores link follows a gentler version of the same pattern, with tooltip amounts around 11,000 to 14,000 dollars in January rising to around 19,000 to 22,000 dollars from mid-February onward, visible as the line growing steadily thicker each time it reappears on the slider.
5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?
Comparing the region borders on active links from two different days suggests the network leans slightly more cross-regional later on. On Day 3, 2 of the 4 active links connect two companies in different regions. On Day 58, 3 of the 5 active links are cross-regional, including links from Ion Systems, a no-border North America company, reaching into both a dashed-border Europe company and solid-border Asia companies. The mix of border styles connected by links does look more varied on later days than on earlier ones.
