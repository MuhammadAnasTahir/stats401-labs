1. Bulletin / Corpus Description

Document: Bulletin of Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction, 2025-2026 (435 pages).
Version: Coordinating edition dated July 2025, covering academic year 2025-2026.
Source: PDF provided for this assignment (ug_bulletin_2025-2026.pdf), issued by the Office of the Registrar.
Date accessed: September 27, 2026.

The bulletin's own Table of Contents lists 12 top-level "Parts" (chapters), covering everything from mission and admissions policy to the full undergraduate curriculum, academic procedures, student life, and a 340-page catalog of majors and course descriptions. The catalog (Part 10: Majors and Courses) alone accounts for roughly 78% of the document's pages.

Pipeline summary: The PDF was parsed with pdfplumber. The Table of Contents (pages 3-9) was parsed first to recover the chapter → section → subsection hierarchy with page numbers. The remaining 426 pages were then walked line-by-line: TOC headings (matched against body text) update the current chapter/section/subsection; individual course headers (regex-matched "CODE ### Title (N credits)" patterns, including cross-listed codes like "SOCIOL 223/PUBPOL 223") start a new passage tagged with that course as the subsection; and, as a fallback for ordinary prose, a vertical-gap heuristic (line-to-line spacing of ~13-14pt within a paragraph vs. ~27-28pt between paragraphs, measured from word bounding boxes) splits new paragraphs. This extracted 2,813 raw passages. Cleaning removed exact duplicates (mostly repeated table headers like "Course Code Course Name Credit" and repeated required-course rows shared across majors), passages under 8 words, and passages that combined course-code-like tokens with no terminal punctuation (a signature of PDF table rows that don't reduce to coherent prose, e.g. requirement-table rows). This left 1,165 cleaned passages across all 12 chapters and 85 distinct chapter/section combinations, averaging 85 words each. Major-requirement tables were the biggest source of noise and are intentionally under-represented in the final corpus; this is discussed further in the Design Description.

5. Semantic Findings

1. What are the major semantic topics?
K-Means (k=12) on the sentence embeddings surfaces topics that mix disciplinary and administrative themes: Degree & Major Requirement Structures (139 passages), History, Literature & Global Cultures (135), Academic Standing, Grading & Registration (128), Math, Science & Physical Education Courses (127), Public Policy, Politics & Environment (125), China & Chinese Language/Culture Studies (108), Arts, Media & Digital Design (107), Student Life, Research & Signature Work (104), DKU Policies, Credit Transfer & Degree Standards (103), plus three smaller, tighter topics: Anthropology, Global Health & Course Updates (42), Tuition, Financial Aid & Costs (31), and a very narrow Quantitative Social Science (Econometrics) cluster (16, dominated by repeated SOSC panel-data course rows).
2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections (chapters)?
DKU Policies, Credit Transfer & Degree Standards is the most widely spread topic, appearing in 9 of the 12 chapters — it captures generic institutional/eligibility language that recurs everywhere from admissions to majors. Student Life, Research & Signature Work (8 chapters), Academic Standing, Grading & Registration (7), China & Chinese Language/Culture Studies (7), and Degree & Major Requirement Structures (7) are also broadly distributed. By contrast, the three smallest topics each live almost entirely inside a single chapter (Part 10), confirming that narrow, jargon-heavy course clusters don't leak into other parts of the bulletin.
3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?
By topic entropy (independent of section size), Part 10: Majors and Courses is the most diverse overall (10 of 12 topics present, entropy 2.98) — unsurprising given its size, but notable because a single chapter spans nearly every discipline offered at DKU. More interesting are two small administrative chapters that turn out to be disproportionately diverse: Part 4: Admission, Scholarships and Financial Aid (only 23 passages, but entropy 2.26 across 5 topics) and Part 8: Career Services, Research Opportunities, and Study Away (35 passages, entropy 2.14 across 5 topics). At the other extreme, Part 7: Academic Advising and Support is completely homogeneous (entropy 0.00; all 11 passages land in the "Student Life, Research & Signature Work" topic).
4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?
The map surfaces several genuine cross-section echoes. DKU's Mission Statement (Part 1) sits near the 21st Century Curriculum description (Part 2) — both restate the same "liberal arts" framing in different chapters. The Statement on Diversity and Inclusion (Part 1) neighbors Campus Programs (Part 9), since both describe DKU's community values. Who We Are (Part 1) neighbors Degree Requirements (Part 3), because both passages describe DKU's relationship to Duke University's academic standards. And the Partners/Wuhan University passage (Part 1) sits near the Study Away section (Part 8), both discussing DKU's network of partner institutions.
5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?
64 passages (about 5.5% of the corpus) have a majority of their nearest semantic neighbors in a different chapter than their own — a proxy for "this doesn't read like the rest of its section." These cluster heavily in Parts 1-3 (General Information, A Liberal Arts Education, The Curriculum): short, early narrative passages introducing DKU's mission and values. Rather than forming their own topic, they scatter into the broader "Student Life" and "DKU Policies" topics — suggesting these opening chapters don't have a distinct semantic voice of their own; they read as a preview of themes developed at length later in the bulletin.
6. Searching "credit", "graduation", "registration", "academic integrity"
These four terms behave very differently. "credit" is pervasive: 697 of 1,165 passages (~60%) mention it, spread across all 12 topics and 8 chapters — it's load-bearing vocabulary everywhere from course descriptions to financial aid. "graduation" is far more concentrated: 42 passages, mostly in the "DKU Policies" and "Academic Standing, Grading & Registration" topics. "registration" is tighter still (17 passages, mostly one topic). Most strikingly, "academic integrity" appears in only 5 passages total, thinly scattered across 5 different topics and just 2 chapters — despite being a named policy section in the bulletin, the literal phrase is rarely repeated in body text, so a keyword search alone would badly under-count how much of the document actually concerns integrity-adjacent conduct policy.

6. Design Description

The corpus is 1,165 cleaned passages extracted from the 435-page DKU bulletin (see Section 1 for the extraction pipeline). Each passage was embedded with sentence-transformers' all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model (384 dimensions, cosine-normalized), then projected to 2D with UMAP (n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.15, cosine metric, seed 401) purely for visual layout — nearby points are similar, but the axes themselves are not interpreted. Topics were generated by K-Means (k=12) on the original 384-dim embeddings (not the 2D projection, to avoid compounding UMAP's distortion), then labeled by hand after reading each cluster's five centroid-nearest passages alongside its top TF-IDF terms; labels blend discipline and function (e.g. "Degree & Major Requirement Structures") because DKU's course catalog structure mixes both. The semantic map encodes position as embedding similarity, color as topic, and point radius as passage length in words — a deliberately restrained channel set so the topic-color pattern stays legible. The Topic×Section matrix instead encodes the bulletin's formal hierarchy (chapter rows) against the same topic labels (columns), letting a viewer see at a glance which topics are chapter-specific vs. bulletin-wide. The two views are coordinated in both directions: clicking a matrix cell sets the map's chapter/topic filters to isolate that cell's passages, and clicking a map point outlines its corresponding matrix cell and opens a detail panel with its five nearest semantic neighbors (each a link back into the map), so a reader can move fluidly between "where is this in the document" and "what else discusses the same idea."
