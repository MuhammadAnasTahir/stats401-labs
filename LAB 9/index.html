1. Dataset Description

Variable: 2025 nominal GDP.
Source: IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025 database. The provided file (data/lab9_gdp_2025_top50.csv) contains the 50 largest economies by nominal GDP in 2025; the IMF notes that WEO figures may include staff estimates and can later be revised.
Geographic boundaries: World country polygons from the datasets/geo-countries project (Natural Earth admin-0 boundaries), joined on ISO-3 codes. France and Norway ship with a placeholder "-99" ISO-3 code in this source and were manually corrected; all other "-99" entries belong to disputed territories (e.g. Western Sahara, Somaliland) that are not in the GDP dataset and are intentionally left as no-data.

Countries in the world boundary file that are not among the provided top-50 economies (the vast majority of the ~190+ sovereign states) are shown with a distinct neutral gray fill in both maps below. This is deliberate as the assignment specifically asks for treating them as missing data, not as zero GDP.

2. 2025 GDP Choropleth

Each country keeps its real geographic shape and position and color encodes 2025 GDP. GDP is extremely right-skewed in this dataset (the largest economy is over 100× the 50th), so a plain linear color scale would make almost every country look identical except the United States and China. The map below therefore uses a logarithmic color scale, which spreads the color gradient across orders of magnitude instead of raw dollars, making differences between e.g. Finland ($315B) and Switzerland ($1,003B) visible alongside the US/China extremes.

3. 2025 GDP Cartogram

This is a Dorling cartogram in which every country becomes a circle placed as close as possible to its true geographic location and circle area represents 2025 GDP. Every overlapping circle is pushed apart by a force simulation. A country's real size and shape are intentionally discarded, only its approximate position and its GDP-driven circle size are kept.

4. Design and Comparison

Both maps use the same 50 countries, the same October 2025 IMF GDP figures, and the same world boundary file joined by ISO-3 code. The only difference is how the data is encoded visually.

In the choropleth, countries with large land area dominate the map regardless of their GDP. Russia, Canada, and Australia rank 9th, 10th, and 15th by GDP respectively, but they take up most of the map's space. Multiple smaller countries have higher GDP yet they are barely visible.

The cartogram corrects this issue. Russia, Canada, and Australia shrink to circles that reflect their actual GDP rank. Meanwhile, geographically small economies like Switzerland, the Netherlands, Singapore, Ireland, Belgium, and Taiwan grow into circles comparable to or larger than Australia's, even though Australia is roughly 350 times their land area.

Each map is better suited to a different kind of question. The choropleth preserves real geography, so it works well for 'where' questions, such as regional GDP patterns or how neighbouring countries compare. The cartogram drops shape and true area entirely, so it works better for 'how much' questions, where land area would otherwise act as a distractor. The difference in perception is sharp. The choropleth makes Russia and Canada look economically dominant in a way their GDP ranks do not support. The cartogram maps area directly onto output, so the United States, China, and some mid-sized European and Asian economies reflect their dominance in the world economy.
